# Theorem — homotopic paths give the same integral of a holomorphic function

The central theorem of the subject, and the reason a contour may be moved
before it is integrated over. Stated here without proof: the source declares
a full proof beyond its scope.

## Statement

> [!theorem] Cauchy's theorem
> Let $G \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ be a [[def-region|region]], let $f$ be [[def-holomorphic|holomorphic]] on $G$, and let $\gamma_0, \gamma_1$ be [[def-piecewise-smooth-path|piecewise smooth]] closed paths in $G$ with $\gamma_0 \sim_G \gamma_1$ ($G$-[[def-homotopic-paths|homotopic]]). Then
> $$
> \int_{\gamma_0} f \; = \; \int_{\gamma_1} f .
> $$

## Notes

> [!note]- Notes (click to expand)
> - Also called the Cauchy–Goursat theorem: the original hypothesis included continuity of $f'$, and removing it is Goursat's contribution.
> - The debt here is the single largest one in this network. Everything downstream is conditional on it.
