# Definition — Jordan curve

A loop that does not cross itself, and nothing more: no smoothness, no
rectifiability. That the theorem below holds at this generality is what makes
it hard — a merely continuous curve can have infinite length, positive area
in every neighbourhood of a point, and no tangent anywhere.

## Statement

> [!definition] Jordan curve
> A *Jordan curve* is the image of a [[def-continuity|continuous]] injective map $r : S^1 \to \mathbb{C}$, where $S^1 = \{ z : |z| = 1 \}$ is the unit [[def-circle-and-disks|circle]].
>
> Equivalently: a subset of the plane homeomorphic to $S^1$, the equivalence holding because $S^1$ is [[def-compact|compact]] and the plane is Hausdorff, so a continuous injection out of $S^1$ is a homeomorphism onto its image.

## Notes

> [!note]- Notes (click to expand)
> - No smoothness is assumed, and none may be: a Jordan curve can have infinite length and no tangent anywhere. Assuming rectifiability or piecewise smoothness would make the theorem easy and would not be this theorem.
